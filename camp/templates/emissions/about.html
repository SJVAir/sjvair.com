    What this covers

    Air pollution reported by permitted stationary sources in the San Joaquin Valley's eight counties, from the California Air Resources Board's facility inventory (CEIDARS), which the air districts report to CARB every year.

    Covers

    • Power plants, glass and cement plants
    • Oil fields and food processors
    • Dairies with permits, landfills, hospitals
    • Gas stations and thousands more

    Doesn't cover

    • Cars and trucks, farm equipment, trains
    • Most farming and dust
    • Consumer products and wildfires
    • Facilities too small to need a permit

    What isn't covered is most of the valley's air pollution; see CARB's estimates of all sources. For the Valley's dairies, permitted or not, see Dairies.

    Two air districts

    Seven of the eight counties are entirely within the San Joaquin Valley Air Pollution Control District. Kern County is split: its desert and mountain east is the Eastern Kern Air Pollution Control District, which regulates Kern's largest cement plants, US Borax at Boron and Edwards Air Force Base. Each facility's page names the district that regulates it and how to report a problem to them.

    CARB's estimates of all sources

    For context, pages compare permitted facilities with CARB's estimate of all emissions in a county: stationary, areawide, mobile and natural sources.

    AreawideFarming, dust, consumer products, residential wood burning
    MobileVehicles and equipment

    The estimates come from CARB's CEPAM model, whose most recent inventory uses 2017 as its base year; every other year is CARB's projection from it, not a new inventory. CARB publishes them in tons per day, which the explorer converts to tons per year.

    Years and changes

    {% if first_year %}The explorer covers {{ first_year }}{% if latest_year != first_year %}–{{ latest_year }}{% endif %}. {% endif %}A large change from one year to the next can reflect a new way of estimating a facility's emissions, not only a real change in what it emitted; facility pages point these out.

    Minor sources

    Gas stations, dry cleaners, auto body and repair shops, small print shops and a few other small trades are permitted, but CARB leaves them out of its facility totals by default and counts them in its county-wide estimates instead. The explorer does the same; turn on Include minor sources to add them.

    Toxicity-weighted emissions

    Pounds alone rank common solvents above diesel particulate and hexavalent chromium, so the explorer's default toxics view weights each pollutant by how toxic it is, the way CARB's own Pollution Mapping Tool does.

    Cancer-weightedpounds × OEHHA inhalation unit risk × molecular-weight adjustment factor × 7,700
    Hazard-weightedpounds ÷ chronic reference exposure level × 0.01712

    A facility's or area's number is shown as its share of the Valley total that year.

    Ammonia

    Ammonia isn't toxic at these levels, but it reacts with other pollution to form fine particles (PM2.5), the Valley's main winter air problem.

    Most Valley ammonia, well over 90% in EPA's estimates, comes from livestock and fertilizer that aren't permitted facilities; only a handful of dairies report ammonia to CARB. County figures are EPA and CARB model estimates for 2023, not measurements.

    Where the ammonia figures come from

    Facility ammonia is what each permitted facility reports to CARB, shown in tons per year beside the criteria pollutants (CARB delivers it in its toxics feed, but the explorer never weights or lists it as a toxic). County and Valley totals by source come from EPA's National Emissions Inventory (NEI), whose California point sources are CARB's own inventory resubmitted: the county pages' ammonia bar and the county dairy pages' dairy-cattle tile are EPA's 2023 estimates by source. {% if nei_stamp %}NEI {{ nei_stamp.version }} loaded.{% else %}No NEI data has been loaded yet.{% endif %}

    Greenhouse gases

    Facility pages for the largest emitters carry a Greenhouse gases card, and county pages a Largest greenhouse-gas reporters table, from EPA's Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program (GHGRP) and CARB's Mandatory GHG Reporting (MRR).

    Greenhouse gases warm the climate; they aren't a local health measure. Only large emitters report, about 10,000 t CO2e a year and up for CARB and 25,000 for EPA, so most facilities have none.

    What isn't reported

    Oil and gas production reports for a whole basin, not one site. Fuel sold by suppliers isn't counted here. Dairies don't report to either program.

    Metric tons

    CO2e is the emitter's own non-biogenic total. CH4 and N2O are tons of the gas, not CO2e; biogenic CO2 (from burning biomass) is shown apart.

    How reporters are matched

    EPA's registry carries the Valley Air District's facility id for most of its reporters, so those matches are exact. CARB's file has only a name, a city and a ZIP; the largest CARB reporters are matched by hand, the rest only when the name clearly matches a facility in the same ZIP. A reporter we couldn't match still appears on its county's table, never on a facility page.

    Years differ

    EPA's newest published year is 2023 (EPA has proposed ending the program); CARB publishes the previous year each November. {% if ghg_stamps.ghgrp or ghg_stamps.mrr %}{% if ghg_stamps.ghgrp %}EPA GHGRP reporting year {{ ghg_stamps.ghgrp.version }}{% if ghg_stamps.mrr %}; {% endif %}{% endif %}{% if ghg_stamps.mrr %}CARB MRR data year {{ ghg_stamps.mrr.version }}{% endif %}.{% else %}No greenhouse-gas data has been imported yet.{% endif %}

    Methane

    The Methane tab and the maps' Methane plumes overlay show the methane point sources in Carbon Mapper's public catalog: plumes seen from aircraft and satellite passes since 2016, clustered into the sites they come from.

    Each source has Carbon Mapper's sector, its estimate of the emission rate and how often it was seen.

    We don't attribute a source to a dairy, facility or operator. A source near a dairy or facility isn't necessarily from it, and which operator a plume belongs to is for researchers and regulators to establish, not for us to infer from the map. Each source is shown by its own location and Carbon Mapper's sector only.

    Plumes are snapshots

    Plumes are snapshots from aircraft and satellite passes. Each rate is an instantaneous estimate with wide uncertainty, not an annual total.

    No plume isn't clean

    A site with no plume hasn't been shown to be clean: it may not have been overflown, or the plume was below detection.

    A climate pollutant

    Methane is a climate pollutant, not a direct local toxic.

    Data by Carbon Mapper®, under Carbon Mapper's non-commercial terms: the sources, plumes and plume images are also available through SJVAir's public API (/api/2.0/emissions/methane/), for non-commercial use with the same attribution and share-alike license carried in every response. Every rate shown is a Carbon Mapper estimate, not SJVAir's.

    Dairies

    The Dairies tab covers what the facility inventory barely sees. CARB's California Dairy & Livestock Database (CADD) locates every dairy CARB tracks, {{ dairy_count|intcomma }} of them in the Valley's {{ county_count }} counties. CEIDARS lists only a handful of permitted livestock facilities.

    Every county, city, community, ZIP area, census tract, school district and AB 617 community has its own dairy page, scoped to that area's dairies. Reach one from its area page, or from the Dairies tab's find box.

    Counted, not modeled. CADD gives each dairy's location, its herd by year ({{ dairy_span }}) and its anaerobic digesters. No public dataset reports emissions dairy by dairy, and the explorer doesn't estimate them.

    Size classes

    Each dairy gets the U.S. EPA's size class for animal feeding operations (40 CFR 122.23) from its counts that year, the larger of the two counts' classes. A Large one is a Large CAFO (concentrated animal feeding operation) by size alone.

    How dairies are counted

    Herd size

    Herds are counted in head: mature dairy cows (milking and dry cows) and other cattle (heifers, calves and beef cattle). A count CADD leaves blank is unknown: it's left out, not counted as zero.

    Reported and estimated counts

    Each count carries a CADD reference code. Code 1 means the count was reported; the other codes (2a–2f, 3a–3g) mark CARB's estimates and gap fills. Dairy popups star the estimated counts.

    Which dairies count in a year

    A dairy counts in a year when CADD has a herd for it that year. A dairy with no cattle counted that year (closed, or not yet open) is left out of it.

    Where a dairy counts

    A dairy counts toward a city or community by its mailing city or its point, whichever falls inside it; it counts toward an urban area, a ZIP area, a census tract, a school district or an AB 617 community by its point only.

    Coverage grew in 2019

    CADD has herds for {{ dairies_before|intcomma }} Valley dairies in 2018 and {{ dairies_after|intcomma }} in 2019. The two years aren't a like-for-like comparison.

    Digesters

    CADD lists the year each digester started and, for the few that closed, the year it shut down. A dairy has a digester in a year when one was operating that year.

    Digester grants

    CDFA's Dairy Digester Research and Development Program lists each grant it has made, the developer, the biogas end use and CDFA's estimate of the annual reduction in metric tons of CO2-equivalent. Dairy popups show a dairy's grants; county dairy pages total them. The reductions are CDFA's estimates, not measurements.

    County dairy emissions

    The Counties view and the county pages use CARB's county inventory (CEPAM) for dairy cattle waste. The explorer shows its ROG, TOG, total PM and PM10; CARB reports no NOx, SOx or CO from dairy cattle, and CEPAM has no ammonia, so the county dairy pages take dairy-cattle ammonia from EPA's NEI. That's the animals and their manure only. Feed and silage are left out: CARB reports them separately, without assigning them to an animal type, and for ROG they're the larger source, so these figures understate dairies' ROG. Like the rest of CEPAM, these are CARB's projections from its 2017 base year, converted from tons per day to tons per year.

    Oil & gas

    CARB's inventory lists oil and gas production as district permit groupings ("HEAVY OIL WESTERN", "LIGHT OIL") that can cover a whole field, and many of Kern's have no map point at all; where one exists it's the operator's address. So the explorer also shows where the wells are.

    That's every active, idle and newly permitted well in CalGEM's WellSTAR database, {{ well_count|intcomma }} in the Valley's counties, {{ kern_well_count|intcomma }} of them in Kern, shown:

    • as an Oil & gas wells overlay on the map,
    • on every area's Oil & gas tab,
    • and as the schools and child-care centers with a well within 3,200 ft, the distance state law (SB 1137) sets for a health protection zone around homes, schools and other sensitive receptors.

    Well counts are CalGEM's regulatory records, not emissions. Idle wells can still leak; plugged wells aren't shown.

    Well types

    CalGEM records what each well is for, in 18 types; the Oil & gas tab groups them four ways.

    Producing

    Oil and gas wells (CalGEM doesn't separate the two) and dry gas wells.

    Injecting to push oil out

    Cyclic steam wells, which steam and then produce in turns, and steamflood, waterflood, pressure-maintenance and air-injection wells, which pump steam, water, gas or air down to drive oil toward producing wells; Kern's heavy oil needs the heat.

    Disposal & gas storage

    Wells that put the salty water from production, or unwanted gas, back underground, other injection wells, and gas storage wells.

    Monitoring & other

    Observation wells, core, dry and stratigraphic holes, water-source and multi-purpose wells, and the few of unknown type.

    Limits of the well data

    Health protection zones are still being revised

    Health-protection-zone status is CalGEM's and is still being revised. Distances to schools are straight lines from the school's map point (the law measures from property lines).

    Permit groupings aren't well locations

    Oil & gas "facilities" in CARB's inventory are permit groupings that can cover a whole field; their map points aren't well locations.

    A snapshot, not a history

    Well data is a snapshot, refreshed weekly from CalGEM. It isn't broken down by year, so it can't show how many wells were active in a past year, and wells plugged since are gone from it. Spud (drilling start) dates are missing for about half of wells. {% if wells_stamp %}Wells last fetched from CalGEM {{ wells_stamp.imported_at|date:"N j, Y" }}.{% else %}No well data has been imported yet.{% endif %}

    Schools and child care

    A facility's page lists the public and private schools and licensed child-care centers within 1,000 ft and within ¼ mile of it, and draws them on the facility's map. An area's Schools tab lists every school and child-care center in it, with the permitted facilities within 1,000 ft, the oil and gas wells within 3,200 ft and the dairies within a mile.

    DistanceWhy that distance
    1,000 ftThe air district must notify parents before permitting a source of hazardous air emissions this close to a school (Health & Safety Code 42301.6)
    ¼ mileWhat school districts must review before siting a school (Education Code 17213)
    3,200 ftCalGEM's health protection zone around oil and gas wells (SB 1137); see Oil & gas
    1 mileDairies, on the Schools tab: a round distance; no law sets one for dairies

    Being nearby isn't a measure of exposure. Distances are straight lines from the school's map point to the facility's, not from property lines.

    Where the sites come from

    The California Department of Education's school directories and the Department of Social Services' child-care licensing list. Child care covers licensed centers only, not family child-care homes.

    When a facility has no list

    The list is left off a facility whose map point can't be trusted to be the site: oil and gas or refinery permit groupings (their addresses are offices, not wells or terminals) and facilities whose address couldn't be matched to a street.

    Compliance

    Facility pages for the largest sources carry a Compliance (federal Clean Air Act reporting) card from EPA's ICIS-Air database, where the Valley Air District reports its inspections, notices of violation, settlements and high-priority violations for the sources federal law requires it to track. {% if compliance_stamp and compliance_stamp.data_through %}Reported through {{ compliance_stamp.data_through|date:"N j, Y" }}.{% else %}No compliance data has been imported yet.{% endif %}

    Only large sources are here. {{ compliance_count|intcomma }} facilities in the Valley's counties are tracked in EPA's air compliance system{% if facility_count %}, against {{ facility_count|intcomma }} that reported to CARB in {{ latest_year }}{% endif %}. Smaller sources' inspections and violations aren't published anywhere. A facility with no compliance card isn't known to be in compliance.

    The record runs late

    The district reports to EPA roughly a year or more behind. Recent quarters look quiet because they haven't been reported yet, not because violations stopped.

    A notice of violation is an allegation

    Many are settled without any admission, and penalties are negotiated amounts.

    Names are as reported to EPA

    They may be a previous owner's.

    How facilities are matched

    The district's facility id is embedded in EPA's id, so the match is exact for the Valley Air District's sources; Eastern Kern sources aren't matched yet. The five-year counts are measured back from the newest event EPA has for that facility, not from today.
