    About this data

    Pesticide Use Reporting (PUR)

    California requires agricultural pesticide applications to be reported to the county agricultural commissioner, who forwards them to the Department of Pesticide Regulation (DPR). DPR checks and publishes each year's records well after the fact, often more than a year later. Each record gives the product, active ingredient, crop or use site, county, one-square-mile section, date, and amount. The commodity list therefore includes non-crop sites alongside crops: rights of way, public health treatments such as mosquito control, structural and landscape work, food processing plants, and commodity fumigation chambers. This explorer loads the records for Fresno, Kern, Kings, Madera, Merced, San Joaquin, Stanislaus, and Tulare counties{% if years %} for {{ years.0 }}–{{ years.1 }}{% endif %}.

    SprayDays notices of intent

    Growers must file a notice of intent before applying a California-restricted material. DPR's SprayDays system publishes those notices 48 hours ahead for fumigants and 24 hours ahead for other restricted materials, and the grower then has up to four days after the scheduled date to begin. Notices cover only restricted materials, list the product and chemical but not the crop, and are not confirmation that the application happened. To be notified about notices near an address, sign up with SprayDays.

    Flagged chemicals

    The scope bar's Flagged chemicals toggle narrows the whole explorer — every count, pounds figure, leaderboard, table and map square — to the active ingredients that appear on at least one of four official lists:

    • Proposition 65: California's list of chemicals known to the state to cause cancer, birth defects or other reproductive harm (see below).
    • CARB Toxic Air Contaminants: air pollutants the California Air Resources Board has identified as posing a health hazard (see below).
    • IARC Groups 1, 2A and 2B: agents the International Agency for Research on Cancer rates as carcinogenic, probably carcinogenic, or possibly carcinogenic to humans (see below). Group 3 is not included.
    • California restricted materials: active ingredients the state restricts under 3 CCR 6400, which need a county permit and a notice of intent before each application (see below).

    A chemical only has to be on one of them. The first three are health-hazard listings and the last is a regulatory control, but a reader asking whether something is one of the bad ones is asking a single question, and a material the state restricts belongs in the answer. The narrower Restricted materials toggle isolates that last list on its own.

    “Flagged” rather than “of concern” because three of these four lists are what that phrase usually names; flagged is the union of all four, and a badge on a chemical anywhere in the explorer means it is on one of them.

    This is the same idea as the chemicals list's Category filter, but broader: that filter picks out a single DPR category (carcinogen, reproductive toxin, toxic air contaminant and so on) one at a time, while the toggle is the union of all four lists above and follows you from page to page. Being listed says a chemical is listed, not how much of it reached anyone — see the caveats below.
